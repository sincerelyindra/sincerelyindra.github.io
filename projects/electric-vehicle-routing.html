Electric-vehicle routing with vehicle-to-grid services

Indra Kumar, Ankur Singh, and Mohd Moin Khan

Project repository ↗

The project studies electric vehicles that serve delivery customers and can optionally discharge energy to the grid. The objective balances travel distance and fleet size against the value of energy services while satisfying routing and operational constraints.

Team project covering mathematical formulation, reinforcement-learning environments, benchmark comparison, and analysis of economic and operational trade-offs.

Approach

  • Formulated a mixed-integer linear programming model with routing, time-window, battery, load, and discharging constraints.
  • Implemented Maskable PPO and a linear Q-learning baseline with feasibility-aware action selection.
  • Evaluated clustered, random, and mixed customer distributions using 100-node Solomon instances extended with discharging stations.
  • Conducted grid-price sensitivity analysis and constructed Pareto trade-offs between distance, fleet size, and energy revenue.

Results

The experiments compare route quality, fleet usage, discharged energy, cost, and runtime. The MILP formulation provided stronger solution quality; reinforcement-learning performance varied with customer distribution and time-window constraints.

Experimental scope

The report identifies incomplete or infeasible RL solutions in some random-distribution cases. The study is a benchmark investigation of the routing methods rather than a claim of deployment in an operating fleet.
