Game theory · Robust optimization

Robust pricing with minimal demand information

Indra Kumar and Prince Kumar

We studied pricing when a seller has only sparse and noisy demand observations. The project reproduces the main computational structure of a robust-pricing framework, tests exact versus interval-valued quantiles, and adds an adaptive confidence-interval rule.

Team project spanning numerical implementation, reproduction experiments, a rolling historical backtest, and an adaptive interval extension.

View code on GitHub

Approach

  • Implemented generalized Pareto interpolation, interval tightening, envelope construction, and numerical robust-price search.
  • Backtested Dominick’s canned-tuna scanner data using 26 training weeks, 6 test weeks, and a 4-week rolling step.
  • Compared fixed 90%, 95%, and 99% confidence intervals with feasibility-based and validation-based adaptive rules.

Results

The robust method led the compared baselines on mean and worst-decile revenue ratios across 1,508 windows. Interval quantiles also maintained feasibility in 99.6–100% of the core feasibility trials.

Experimental scope

Revenue ratios are historical backtest measures, evaluated at the nearest observed future price. The solver uses numerical approximations, the demand inputs include a quantity-based proxy, and the synthetic benchmark produced mixed results.
